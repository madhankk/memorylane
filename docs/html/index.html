For anyone with a growing archive

Let old memories surface again.

Most photos in a large library never get looked at again after the day they were taken. MemoryLane's home screen surfaces one at random every time you open it, alongside a slideshow of what happened on this day in years past.

For libraries that never stop growing

Built for large
photography hoards.

A library of 140,000 photos across 550 folders still renders in under a second, because MemoryLane keeps its own small index next to your files instead of scanning them on every visit. RAW and JPEG pairs from the same shot link automatically, so they never show up as two separate photos, and full bursts collapse the same way. When you want more control, select any set of photos yourself and stack them by hand.

Screenshot: folder view at scale

  • Fast browsing at any size, from a few hundred photos to hundreds of thousands.
  • RAW and JPEG pairs from the same shot link automatically, with no separate import step.
  • Select any photos yourself and stack them by hand, on top of the automatic grouping.
  • Browse and filter by the keyword tags already embedded in your files.

Every format you actually shoot

Video, and Live Photos,
handled properly.

Video is indexed with a poster-frame thumbnail and a duration badge, and plays the original file directly in your browser's own player. Nothing is transcoded automatically, so playback depends only on what your browser can already decode. Apple Live Photos are detected on their own, shown as one tile with a LIVE badge, and play their paired clip right in the grid.

Screenshot: video tile
poster frame, duration badge, native playback

Screenshot: Live Photo tile
LIVE badge, playing inline

  • Old camera formats that won't play in a browser can be flagged and converted, opt-in, from Settings.
  • You review and confirm the converted result before anything happens to the original.
  • The original is only ever moved into a plain folder next to it, never deleted.

Optional, and local either way

AI features you start
yourself, on your own
hardware.

Find visually similar photos, search with a description instead of a filename, or group faces into people you can name. All three run through a local sidecar process that never sends a file path anywhere, only the thumbnails it needs to do its work.

Local-first, always

Your photos stay
where you put them.

MemoryLane is something you run, not a service you sign up for. There's no account beyond the admin login you create the first time you start it, no cloud upload step, and no subscription behind any of its features.

  • MemoryLane only ever reads your originals. The index and thumbnail cache it builds alongside them are disposable, and deleting them just means the next scan rebuilds everything from scratch.
  • The one narrow exception: an opt-in tool that converts old video formats browsers can't play, and even that only moves the original into a plain folder next to it, after you've reviewed the result, never deleting anything.
  • If you turn on the AI features, the sidecar still never sees a file path, only the thumbnails it needs.

Ready when you are.

Point it at a folder and it starts indexing. Everything above happens on its own from there.
